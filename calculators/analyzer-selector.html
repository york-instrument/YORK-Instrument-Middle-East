      How the selector chooses

      • Range first. Each recommendation covers the driest dew point or lowest oxygen level you expect, with margin.
      • Chilled mirror or metal oxide. Chilled mirror instruments measure the dew point directly and suit reference work and audits. Metal-oxide sensors reach −110 °C frost point, respond quickly and suit continuous monitoring and natural gas.
      • Electrochemical or paramagnetic. Electrochemical cells reach trace levels (ppm). Paramagnetic sensors suit percent levels and have no consumable cell.
      • Hazardous areas. Explosion-proof versions are listed where available. Certification documents are provided per model.

      The shortlist is a starting point. Sample conditions such as pressure, temperature, contaminants and flow decide the final configuration, so send us your answers and we will confirm.

      Frequently asked questions

      Should I choose a chilled mirror or a metal-oxide dew point meter?

      Choose a chilled mirror when you need a fundamental, traceable measurement, for example as a reference or for audits. Choose a metal-oxide sensor for continuous monitoring, very dry gases down to −110 °C frost point, or natural gas where hydrocarbons can condense on a mirror.

      Which oxygen sensor is right for trace levels?

      For ppm-level oxygen in nitrogen, argon or other inert gases, an electrochemical trace oxygen analyzer is the usual choice. YORK trace oxygen analyzers cover 0.01 ppm to 25 % O₂.

      Do you have explosion-proof analyzers?

      Yes, explosion-proof versions are available for dew point and trace oxygen. Certification documents are provided per model, so tell us the zone, gas group and temperature class.
