How the calculator works

The water vapour pressure is calculated from the dew point with the Sonntag (1990) equations: over ice below 0 °C (frost point), or over water if you tick the box. Water content in ppmV is the vapour pressure divided by the total pressure. Because the ppmV of a gas does not change when it is compressed or expanded, the calculator uses it to find the dew point at any other pressure.

What about temperature?

The gas temperature does not change the dew point or the water content: both depend only on how much water is in the gas and on the pressure. Temperature matters for what that water does. Enter the gas or pipe temperature to see the relative humidity, the margin between the temperature and the dew point, and whether water will condense. In the Gulf this is common where compressed air leaves a hot compressor room and runs through air-conditioned buildings or cooler night-time pipework.

Example

Compressed air at 7 bar(g) with a pressure dew point of −40 °C holds about 16 ppmV of water. Expanded to atmospheric pressure, the same air has a dew point of about −57 °C.

Limits

  • By default ideal-gas mixing is assumed. The enhancement factor (Buck 1996) corrects for real-gas behaviour of water vapour. It is defined near atmospheric pressure; above about 10 bar it is an extrapolation and gives an approximate correction.
  • lb/MMscf and mg/Sm³ are ideal-gas conversions. For natural gas at pipeline pressure, the water content at a given dew point differs from the ideal-gas value; use ISO 18453 (GERG-water) for custody transfer.
  • ppmW depends on the molar mass of the background gas; choose the gas from the list.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert pressure dew point to atmospheric dew point?

Calculate the water content in ppmV from the pressure dew point and the line pressure, then find the dew point that gives the same ppmV at atmospheric pressure. For example, −40 °C at 7 bar(g) is about 16 ppmV, which is about −57 °C at atmospheric pressure.

Why is the pressure dew point higher than the atmospheric dew point?

Compressing a gas raises the partial pressure of its water vapour in proportion to the total pressure, so the gas becomes saturated at a higher temperature. The water content in ppmV stays the same.

Does gas temperature affect the dew point?

No. The dew point and the water content depend on the amount of water and on the pressure, not on the gas temperature. The temperature decides whether that water condenses: if the gas or pipe is colder than the dew point, water will condense. Enter the temperature in the calculator to check the margin.

Can I use this for natural gas?

Yes, as an indication. The lb/MMscf and mg/Sm³ values are ideal-gas conversions; for natural gas at high pressure use ISO 18453 or your contract method for the final figure.
